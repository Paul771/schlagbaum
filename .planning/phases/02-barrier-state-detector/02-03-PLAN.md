<objective>
Harden the proven detector against the conditions real footage will bring, wire it into the operator's config surface, and make the SC4 dependency on real recorded footage explicit and non-skippable.

Three threads, all expansions from the tracer: robustness tests that subject the detector to frame degradation, replay determinism, and the Pitfall 1 bounded-buffer deadlock; the operator-facing configuration surface (ROI keys in `config.json`, startup bounds validation, an offline ROI overlay renderer, and the `fixtures/` ignore entry); and the user-gated SC4 acceptance path.

Live capture is blocked on this host, and the footage has to be recorded by a person standing at the gate. This plan makes that dependency impossible to overlook: the footage request is a blocking human checkpoint, the ignore entry lands before anything can be recorded, and the acceptance test fails loudly rather than skipping when footage is missing. Nothing here claims SC4 while the footage is absent.

Write the three tests first.

Test 1 renders one clip and re-derives the ratio from three degraded variants: a downscale-and-restore, a low-quality JPEG round trip, and frames with seeded additive noise. Assert the same three-value ordering and the same terminal state in all three. Cite RESEARCH's conservative margin (0.184, closed 0.438 / open-family 0.254) alongside the typical one (0.284) in the inline comment, since the conservative sweep's HALF45 reading of 0.254 sits above release=0.25 and is the number that shows how little headroom the degraded cases actually have. This is the test that fails if the detector ever becomes sensitive to encoding artefacts rather than to arm geometry.

Test 2 runs ReplaySource over the same directory twice, collects the emitted transition records from both runs, and asserts the two collections are equal as whole objects. Because the FSM has no time source, replaying is expected to be exactly reproducible; making the comparison structural rather than length-based is what catches a frame-index drift or a field that is stable in count but not in value.

Test 3 is the Pitfall 1 regression guard, and its design matters more than its assertion. FrameBuffer.pop() is a bare blocking queue get with no timeout, so a replay that pushes more frames than the buffer holds while a single consumer is not draining fast enough deadlocks permanently. Structure the test so that this is impossible: drive the replay through a bounded buffer with a consumer that takes frames with a timeout-carrying get, and treat an exhausted timeout as the assertion failure. Use an explicit sentinel for end-of-clip rather than relying on the generator ending. Do not attempt to detect the hang with a wall clock, and do not rely on pytest's faulthandler diagnostic to terminate it — that option only dumps tracebacks. Assert the guard's boundedness inside this same test — a plain assertion that the test's own frame budget is finite — so the property is visible without adding a fourth name to the module. If a future editor converts the guard back to a bare blocking get, this test's timeout assertion fails immediately rather than wedging the suite, and that is the property being documented.

Do not modify src/capture/frame_buffer.py. Phase 1 delivered and tested that contract; this plan consumes it. If a real defect in it is discovered, that is a finding to report, not a silent edit in a Phase 2 plan.
